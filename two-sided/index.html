Two-sided experiment design: when treatment changes control

Concept

An A/B test assigns units to treatment or control and compares outcomes. The desired estimand is the quantity the experiment aims to measure, such as the difference between full rollout and no rollout. It need not equal the difference seen while treatment and control share a marketplace.

Interference means one unit's outcome depends on other units' assignments. Johari et al. model a two-sided market and compare customer, listing, and two-sided randomization. Their analysis connects bias to market balance and shows why no single side's naive randomization is universally reliable. Paper, Management Science 2022; preprint 2020.

Architecture

This diagram describes experimental relationships, not a neural-network architecture. A larger sample can reduce variance without removing design bias.

Worked example

Imagine two buyers compete for the last available appointment. A treatment recommendation helps buyer A book it first. Buyer B, assigned control, now has one fewer option. B's outcome changed despite B's experience using the control algorithm. A treatment-control difference includes this competition effect.

More buyers than appointments is a different environment from many unbooked appointments and few buyers. Which side is scarce changes how treatment propagates. In ads, scarce resources can include budget and impression supply; do not mechanically transfer every appointment-market assumption to auctions.

Implementation exercise

Write a tiny matching simulator with buyers, listings, capacities, and a treatment that changes choice probabilities. First measure all-control and all-treatment outcomes to define a simulated full-rollout effect. Then measure a mixed customer-randomized experiment and a mixed listing-randomized experiment.

Repeat runs at several supply/demand ratios with fixed parameters and many random seeds. Plot experimental estimates against the simulated rollout effect, including uncertainty. Label this an educational simulator; it cannot establish unbiasedness for a real product or replace the paper's assumptions and proofs.

Check your understanding

Which resource couples units? What outcome would full rollout change? Why does collecting ten times as much data not automatically fix interference bias?
