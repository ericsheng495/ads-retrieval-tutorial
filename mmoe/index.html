MMoE: each task chooses its shared experts

Concept

Imagine predicting both clicks and purchases. A shared representation can reuse useful information, but the tasks may disagree. A click-seeking representation need not be the best purchase representation. This is the motivation for flexible sharing rather than one mandatory shared trunk.

MMoE has multiple shared expert subnetworks. Each task has its own input-dependent gate, usually a softmax, that mixes expert outputs before its prediction tower. The experts can be shared while task-specific gates select different mixtures. Ma et al., KDD 2018.

Architecture

For task t, the representation is sum_i gate_t(i | x) * expert_i(x). The gate probabilities sum to one across experts. They are routing weights, not probabilities that a user clicks or converts.

Worked example

Two experts output invented vectors e1=[1,0] and e2=[0,2]. The click gate uses weights [0.8,0.2], producing [0.8,0.4]. The purchase gate uses [0.3,0.7], producing [0.3,1.4]. Different prediction heads consume those vectors.

Both tasks can use both experts. The experts do not need manually assigned roles such as “click expert.” Whether specialization develops is an empirical question; a gate becoming nearly constant is something to inspect.

Implementation exercise

Build two small expert MLPs returning [batch,d], two gates returning [batch,2], and two heads. Stack expert outputs as [batch,experts,d], multiply by a gate expanded along d, and sum the expert axis. Verify the toy mixtures.

Define each task's labels, valid-example mask, and loss before adding the weighted total loss. Compare separate models, shared-bottom, and MMoE with matched budgets. Report both task metrics: an average can hide a regression. An all-impression purchase label differs from conversion conditional on a click.

Check your understanding

Why is there a separate gate per task? Does a click gate weight of 0.8 imply an 80% click probability? What evidence would show negative transfer?
