Budget-split design: isolate the resource that couples groups

Concept

Suppose treatment and control users compete for spending from the same campaign. A model that spends more in treatment can leave less budget for control. The groups interact through that shared resource even if user assignments are random.

Liu, Mao, and Kang propose budget-split experimentation: split the relevant buyer/campaign budget across experimental marketplaces associated with randomized seller/member partitions. The design's causal guarantees depend on its stated market and allocation assumptions. The mechanism is resource isolation, not merely adding a budget column to a results table. Paper, 2020 preprint.

Architecture

The key contrast is a common spending pool versus per-arm accounting and eligibility. A spending debit in one isolated arm should not reduce another arm's budget allocation under the design.

Worked example

An invented campaign has budget 100 units. With one shared pool, treatment could spend 80 and leave at most 20 for control. A naive comparison would mix the model's effect with unequal access to budget.

For equally sized experimental partitions, allocate 50 to each arm. If treatment spends 50, control's allocation remains 50. For a 20%/80% traffic split, a toy proportional allocation would be 20/80 rather than 50/50. Actual design requires consistent population, allocation, pacing, and outcome definitions.

This example illustrates accounting isolation. It does not prove that splitting budgets eliminates every form of marketplace interference or that rollout effects can be inferred without checking assumptions.

Implementation exercise

Build a two-arm ledger: campaign ID, initial per-arm budget, remaining budget, and spend transactions. Assert that arm-A spend cannot mutate arm-B's balance. Simulate requests with arm assignment, available ads, and a simple spending rule. Compare the common-pool and isolated-ledger versions under the same request stream.

Report per-arm allocation, delivery, spend, and outcomes. Include a low-demand case where budgets do not bind. Then write what remains unmodeled: changing advertiser behavior, pacing adaptation, cross-campaign substitution, and delayed outcomes. Keep a simulation demonstration separate from an unbiasedness claim.

Check your understanding

Which shared state must be separated? Why should allocation reflect the design's traffic/population split? What extra mechanisms could still couple the arms?
