Better negative sampling

Concept

Training against sampled candidates changes the competition each positive faces. In-batch candidates reflect the interaction distribution, which often favors popular items. Uniform corpus sampling gives different coverage. Hard negatives are candidates the current model mistakenly scores highly.

Sampling correction, exposure bias, and hard-example selection are different problems. Correcting the candidate sampler does not make logged feedback representative of items that users never had a chance to see.

A negative is not always an observed rejection

A shown nonclick differs from an unshown item sampled as a training contrast. A false negative is a contrasted item that would actually be relevant. We usually cannot identify all such items without observing more outcomes.

Uniform sampling gives equal draw probabilities to catalog items. In-batch sampling inherits item frequencies from the example stream. Hard sampling searches for alternatives the model currently finds competitive. These choices change the learning signal as well as compute cost.

Think of practice questions: absurd alternatives teach little, but calling a second correct answer wrong teaches a contradiction. Harder candidates help only when the supervision and sampling assumptions remain appropriate.

Worked example

Under a simplified sampler, item A has sampling probability Q(A)=0.5 and B has Q(B)=0.1. Equal raw training logits of 2 become approximately 2.693 and 4.303 after subtracting log Q. The less frequently sampled candidate receives a larger correction. These are corrected training logits, not a general recipe for changing serving scores.

If half your negative draws are uniform over N items and half come from q_batch, the marginal probability of a draw is 0.5/N + 0.5*q_batch(i). That alone does not settle how to correct the full loss: expected counts, repeated columns, positive inclusion, and the sampling process must also be accounted for.

Application to this project

Our v0 has duplicate and known-positive handling. Its multi-positive probability mass objective is not automatically the objective in Yi or Yang. Derive the assumptions of your extension, especially before adding a correction to every numerator/denominator column. Use a tiny catalog where full scores are affordable to check the behavior before a MovieLens sweep.

Implement it

  1. Count item frequencies using training data only; inspect the head/tail imbalance.
  2. Write a sampler that returns candidate IDs plus probabilities or expected counts.
  3. Derive your corrected training objective, including duplicates, on a three-item catalog.
  4. Test the uniform special case: a common correction to every logit should cancel in softmax.
  5. Add uniform corpus negatives at a fixed total candidate budget.
  6. Mine high-scoring unobserved items with a training-only checkpoint; mask known positives.
  7. Compare separate ablations for correction, mixture, hard negatives, and batch size.

Track overall and head/tail recall, epoch time, memory, and a qualitative audit of plausible false negatives. Read Yi, Yang, Facebook EBR, and the optional cross-batch paper in the guide. This phase is an exercise, not an implemented feature of the reference trainer.

Self-check
