Snap's ranking references: a guided learning path

Start with recommender foundations and how ranking works. You do not need to read ten papers before building anything. These lessons introduce the missing concepts first.

Where the references fit

Snap's 2022 engineering post connects candidate generation, heavy prediction models, auctions, and feedback. It also covers feature pipelines, training, continuous updates, experimentation, and monitoring. The references below support different parts of that story; being cited does not establish today's implementation of a particular Snap team.

Eight references concern prediction/modeling; two concern experiment design. A better architecture and a trustworthy estimate of its business effect are separate requirements. None of these rankers must replace your two-tower retriever.

Prerequisite: what is a feature interaction?

Let H=1 mean “recent hiking interest” and A=1 mean “hiking ad.” A linear score b + wH*H + wA*A gives the hiking-interest effect the same increment regardless of the ad. Adding wHA*H*A lets the combination receive an additional effect.

H A H×A Meaning
0 0 0 Neither signal
1 0 0 Interest without a matching ad
0 1 0 Hiking ad without observed interest
1 1 1 Their combination is active

For categorical values, manual crosses can explode in number: 1,000 interests times 10,000 ad categories produces 10 million possible pairs. Sparse observations make independent coefficients unreliable for rare combinations. Embeddings, factorization, neural branches, and cross layers offer different sharing rules.

An explicit interaction appears through a designed product operation. An implicit interaction may be represented by a flexible nonlinear network without a separately named product feature. “Explicit” is about the computation; it does not automatically make a model causally interpretable.

Prerequisite: multi-task learning

Predicting clicks and purchases are two tasks. Separate models cannot share what they learn. A shared-bottom network shares an early representation, then uses a different prediction head per task. That can help scarce labels but can also cause negative transfer: optimizing one task worsens the other.

MMoE and PLE change how representations are shared. Neither fixes mismatched label definitions, incomplete conversion windows, or sampling bias by itself. Always specify whether the conversion task is per impression or conditional on a click, and which examples are valid for each loss.

How to implement without turning this into ten projects

Build one tiny ranker harness: identical feature encoding, chronological split, batching, loss, metrics, and model interface. Swap one architecture at a time. For the first experiments use an explicitly synthetic feature-interaction task. For realistic CTR evaluation, use an appropriate impression dataset and its terms. MovieLens ratings do not constitute an ads CTR or conversion benchmark.

For each paper, the lesson gives a minimal exercise, not a claim that the entire paper has been reproduced in src/. Record log loss, AUC where defined, calibration, serving cost, and task slices before making architecture claims.
